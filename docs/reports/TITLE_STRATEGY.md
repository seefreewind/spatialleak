## Top 3 Recommendations

### 1. SpatialLeak reveals distinct spatial-neighborhood and patient-level shortcuts in spatial omics prediction

Best for: Nature Communications, Genome Biology.

Why: It foregrounds the two-channel novelty and names the field and task. It is assertive but bounded.

Risk: "shortcuts" may need definition in the Abstract and Introduction to avoid sounding accusatory.

### 2. SpatialLeak: a leakage-resistant benchmark for spatial omics prediction

Best for: Bioinformatics, Briefings in Bioinformatics, JBI.

Why: It is concise, searchable, and benchmark-centered.

Risk: It undersells the two-channel conceptual contribution.

### 3. Leakage-resistant evaluation separates local spatial dependence from patient-associated shortcuts in spatial omics prediction

Best for: Genome Biology, methodologically oriented computational biology journals.

Why: It is precise and states the main conceptual move without leaning entirely on the SpatialLeak brand.

Risk: Slightly long; less memorable than a SpatialLeak-led title.

## Avoided Title Types

- Titles claiming all spatial omics studies are invalid.
- Titles implying causal proof of every leakage mechanism.
- Titles using "AI" as a broad hook.
- Titles using "bias" instead of the defined leakage/inflation terminology.
